# A Road Trip

> Reach for this one if you think in journeys and logistics.

## Differentiation

A road trip has genuinely separate components: the vehicle (needs fuel,
maintenance, capacity), the route (the actual roads and turns), the
schedule (when you leave, when you need to arrive), the stops (fuel,
food, rest, sights), and, if you're not travelling alone, the other
people — who each have their own needs and preferences for all of the
above. A great car and a great route are not, by themselves, a trip.

## Linkage

The plan is the linkage: timing the fuel stops to the car's range and the
route's terrain, not just "we'll stop when we're low"; sequencing the
stops so rest and food land where the drive actually gets long, not on a
fixed clock; communicating with everyone in the car about the plan so a
change (traffic, weather, someone needing a break) gets absorbed instead
of causing a fight forty-five minutes later.

## Emergent property

Actually arriving — together, on reasonable terms, having maybe even
enjoyed it. Not "a car that runs" plus "a map that's correct" sitting
next to each other, but a trip: those things working as one connected
thing across time.

## What breaks

**Parts without linkage:** a great route mapped out and a full tank of
gas, planned completely separately, so nobody actually checked whether
the gas would last the distance between stops on that particular route.
The parts were each fine. Nobody linked them.

**Linkage without real differentiation:** planning every single minute
with zero slack, so one fifteen-minute delay collapses the whole
schedule and everyone's stressed for the rest of the day. Real linkage
leaves room for the parts to flex — a route that can absorb a stop
running long is more integrated, not less, than one planned down to the
minute. Rigid isn't the same as connected.

## Try it

On your next trip — or your next project — ask where the plan has zero
slack. That's usually not "well-integrated," it's just brittle, and it'll
show the first time reality doesn't cooperate.
