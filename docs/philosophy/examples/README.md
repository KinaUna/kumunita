# Examples — One Door Per Reader

[`everyday-life.md`](../everyday-life.md) makes the case with three
examples (a meal, a bicycle, a relay race) and stops there — it's meant to
be read start to finish in ten minutes. This folder is different: it's not
a reading path, it's a shelf. Everyone's background makes some examples
click instantly and leaves others flat — a nurse and a mechanic won't
reach for the same one. Pick whichever one already lives in your head.

Every doc here is the same shape:

- **Differentiation** — the parts, and the different job each one does.
- **Linkage** — what actually connects them; the part that's rare and easy
  to skip.
- **Emergent property** — what only the working whole produces; no part
  has it alone.
- **What breaks** — both failure directions: parts with no linkage between
  them, and linkage that erases the differentiation instead of connecting
  it.

None of these require jargon or a technical background. Read one, then
run the [three tests](../START-HERE.md#the-three-tests) — closed-loop?
handoff? part-vs-whole? — on the thing it reminded you of in your own
life or work.

## Using these with someone new

If you're introducing Fractal Integration to someone who doesn't live in
the working vocabulary yet, this folder is often a better door in than
`everyday-life.md` itself: ask what they already spend their time on, hand
them the matching example, and let them find the pattern before you ever
say the word "integration." Once it clicks once, it's easy to point at the
next scale — [`START-HERE.md`](../START-HERE.md) — and say: same shape,
bigger system.

More examples are welcome. A good addition names its parts honestly (not
padded to fit), finds a linkage that's easy to overlook, and shows both
ways the example can fail — that's what makes it teach the whole idea, not
just illustrate a nice metaphor.
