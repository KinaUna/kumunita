# A Calendar

> Reach for this one if you think in schedules and time.

## Differentiation

Your week is made of different kinds of time, and they aren't
interchangeable: focused work, meetings, travel between places, meals,
exercise, sleep, time with people who matter to you. A calendar's job
starts by telling these apart — an hour of deep work and an hour of
commuting are not the same unit just because they're both "an hour."

## Linkage

A list of events isn't a calendar yet. The linkage is everything that
turns separate appointments into a schedule you can actually run your
life on: the sequencing (what has to come before what), the buffers
between events (time to travel, to reset, to actually eat), the recurring
patterns that hold a shape week to week, and the shared visibility that
lets other people's time link to yours — invites, availability, reminders
that fire before it's too late to react.

## Emergent property

Time you can trust. Not "I have a calendar" but "I know where I need to
be, I'm not double-booked, and the gaps I see are real gaps I can use."
No single event gives you that — it comes only from the whole schedule
holding together.

## What breaks

**Parts without linkage:** appointments scattered across sticky notes, a
notes app, someone's verbal "let's say Tuesday," and your memory — each
one fine on its own, none of them talking to the others. The conflict
doesn't show up until you're standing in two places at once.

**Linkage without real differentiation:** a calendar packed back-to-back
with zero buffer, every block bleeding into the next. It looks organized
— every hour is "accounted for" — but it can't survive one thing running
long, and it treats a focus block and a five-minute stand-up as the same
kind of slot. The other version of this failure is the giant "misc" or
"admin" block that swallows everything uncategorized — nothing in it ever
gets its own moment, so nothing in it ever quite gets done.

## Try it

Look at tomorrow. Is there a gap that's actually protected, or is every
"free" slot really just "not yet booked"? That's the three tests, run on
your own week: closed-loop, handoff, part-vs-whole.
