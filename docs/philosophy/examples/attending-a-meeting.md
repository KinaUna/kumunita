# Attending a Meeting

> Reach for this one if you spend your week in meetings.

## Differentiation

A good meeting has different things doing different jobs: an agenda that
states what decision or output the time is for, attendees who each bring
something distinct (a facilitator to steer, a note-taker to capture, a
decision-maker who can actually say yes, subject experts who know things
nobody else in the room does), and often pre-read material that lets
people show up already informed instead of using the meeting itself to
get up to speed.

## Linkage

The facilitation is the linkage: opening with the purpose so everyone
knows what "done" looks like, keeping the discussion on the actual
question instead of every adjacent one, timeboxing so one topic doesn't
eat the rest, drawing out the quiet expert instead of letting the loudest
voice fill the room, and — critically — capturing the decision and the
action items, then distributing them afterward so the meeting's output
survives past the moment people close their laptops.

## Emergent property

A decision made, or a shared understanding that didn't exist walking in.
That's the entire point of gathering people instead of emailing them — an
outcome no single attendee had on their own, produced by the room working
together, not by any one person's individual expertise.

## What breaks

**Parts without linkage:** everyone talks, opinions surface, something
that feels like a decision gets made out loud — and then nobody wrote it
down. By next week it's evaporated, or worse, three people remember it
three different ways. A signal with no loop: the meeting generated
output, but nothing carried it forward.

**Linkage without real differentiation:** one person did all the talking
and everyone else was an audience. Attendance isn't the same as
contribution — if the room's different expertise never actually got
pulled in, you didn't need a room, you needed that one person's opinion
sent by email. This is the "god part" failure wearing a conference-room
badge.

## Try it

After your next meeting, run the closed-loop test: did the decision leave
the room attached to an owner and a next step, or is it still floating,
waiting to be "circled back on"?
