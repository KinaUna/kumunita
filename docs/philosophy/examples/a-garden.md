# A Garden

> Reach for this one if you'd rather think about plants than people.

## Differentiation

A garden bed full of plants is not one thing — it's many different
organisms with different needs. A tomato wants full sun, steady water,
and support to climb. A rosemary wants poor soil, little water, and to be
left alone. Basil wants warmth and regular pinching back. Treating a
garden as "plants" instead of "this specific plant, and that specific
plant" is where a garden starts to fail before it's even planted.

## Linkage

The linkage is the gardener's ongoing attention: watering matched to what
each plant actually needs rather than a single schedule for the whole
bed, companion planting where some species genuinely help their
neighbors (basil near tomatoes, deep roots next to shallow ones),
seasonal timing — planting and pruning at the point in the year each
species is ready for, not on one fixed calendar for the whole garden.

## Emergent property

A garden that thrives — food, flowers, or just a space that's alive and
worth being in. Not "a collection of plants that are each, individually,
technically not dead," but a bed where the plants' different needs are
being met well enough, and often enough helping each other, that the
whole thing flourishes.

## What breaks

**Parts without linkage:** seeds or seedlings put in the ground and then
left — no attention paid to who needs what. Some survive by luck, most
struggle or die, and the garden never becomes more than the sum of
whatever happened to make it.

**Linkage without real differentiation:** watering everything on the
exact same schedule regardless of species, because it's simpler than
learning each plant's actual needs. It looks like care — you're watering
every day! — but it drowns the rosemary and still isn't enough for the
tomato. Real linkage means connecting to what each part actually is, not
applying one rule and calling it attention.

## Try it

Look at what you're treating identically that actually isn't. That gap is
usually where the "differentiation is deliberate" principle got skipped.
