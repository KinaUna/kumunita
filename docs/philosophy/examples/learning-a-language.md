# Learning a Language

> Reach for this one if you've ever studied a skill from scratch.

## Differentiation

Fluency is made of distinct sub-skills that don't substitute for each
other: vocabulary (knowing the words), grammar (knowing how they combine),
listening (parsing fast, real, messy speech), speaking (producing sound
under time pressure, not just recognizing it), and pronunciation (being
understood). Someone can be strong in one and genuinely weak in another —
they're different capacities, not one skill wearing different names.

## Linkage

The linkage is practice that actually connects the sub-skills to each
other: using new vocabulary inside real sentences instead of flashcards in
isolation, having conversations where you get corrected in the moment,
listening to speech you then have to respond to rather than just absorb.
This is what turns "I know words" and "I know rules" into "I can talk to
a person" — the input-output loop, with feedback, is the part that links
knowledge to actual ability.

## Emergent property

Fluency — the ability to actually communicate in real time, under
pressure, with another person. That's not the sum of vocabulary size plus
grammar knowledge; plenty of people can pass a grammar test and freeze in
a real conversation. It only shows up when the sub-skills are integrated
under real conditions.

## What breaks

**Parts without linkage:** memorizing two thousand flashcards and never
having a conversation. The vocabulary is real, but it never became
language — it stayed a pile of disconnected parts, because the thing that
links them (use, under real conditions, with feedback) never happened.

**Linkage without real differentiation:** trying to "just absorb it" by
immersion alone with no structure or feedback loop — hoping exposure
substitutes for actually building the parts. Some of that works, but
without ever isolating grammar or pronunciation to work on them
deliberately, errors calcify because nothing is closing the loop and
telling you they're wrong.

## Try it

Notice which sub-skill you're avoiding practicing because it's
uncomfortable. That's usually the seam where your fluency is actually
capped — not the part you keep drilling because you're already good at
it.
