# Digesting Food

> Reach for this one if biology or health makes sense to you. Simplified
> on purpose — it's a shape to think with, not a physiology lesson.

## Differentiation

Digestion isn't one organ doing one job; it's a chain of specialists, each
doing something the others can't. Your mouth breaks food down mechanically
and starts one kind of chemical breakdown. Your stomach adds acid and a
different set of enzymes. Your pancreas contributes enzymes tuned for fats,
proteins, and carbohydrates. Your small intestine is where most of the
actual absorption happens. Your liver processes what gets absorbed before
it reaches the rest of the body. None of these could do what the whole
system does — a stomach alone doesn't give you energy; it gives you a
churned mess without absorption.

## Linkage

The parts only work because they're sequenced and cued correctly.
Chewing has to happen before swallowing, or the stomach receives pieces it
can't compensate for. The pancreas releases the right enzymes because
signals — nerves and hormones — tell it food has arrived and roughly what
kind. The stomach empties into the small intestine at a controlled pace,
not all at once. This timing and signaling is the linkage: it's not
tissue, it's coordination between tissues.

## Emergent property

Usable nutrients — energy, the raw material for growth and repair. Food
itself doesn't have that; a sandwich sitting on a plate is not "energy,"
it's potential that only becomes real through the whole working chain. No
single organ produces it; only the integrated sequence does.

## What breaks

**Parts without linkage:** a stage does its own job perfectly, but the
handoff to the next stage fails — food swallowed too fast, in pieces too
large for the stomach to compensate for, straining everything downstream.
Each organ "passed its own test," but the seam between them is where the
system actually suffers — the same shape as code that's green in every
unit test but breaks the moment two services talk to each other.

**Linkage without real differentiation:** trying to skip the stages
instead of connecting them — as if blending every meal into a uniform
paste would make digestion easier, when what the body actually needed was
each stage doing its own distinct job, in order. Erasing the
differentiation isn't the same as integrating it.

## Try it

Next time something in a process "should have worked" but didn't, ask
where the equivalent of chewing happened too fast for what came next —
that's usually where to look first.
